Within-Piraeus segment comparison

Container versus non-container tonnage inside Piraeus, with no external donor ports. Treatment begins in 2016 Q4; 2016 Q3 is excluded.

Short answer

This design asks whether Piraeus container traffic improved relative to Piraeus non-container traffic after the ownership transfer. Quarter fixed effects absorb shocks common to both segments inside the port.

Growth columns compare geometric-average post-treatment tonnage with geometric-average pre-treatment tonnage. The DiD is their relative change, not their percentage-point difference. The seasonal specification adds quarter-of-year indicators to the log cargo ratio. Newey–West HAC inference uses four quarterly lags.

How it is calculated

log(tonnages,t) = segment FE + quarter FE + β(Container × Post) + error

The coefficient β is exactly equal to:

average post log(Container / Non-container)
− average pre log(Container / Non-container)

The dashboard reports 100 × [exp(β) − 1]. The explicit fixed-effects regression and the collapsed log-ratio calculation agree to numerical precision.

Event-study normalization

With average-pre normalization, the average post-treatment event-study effect is mechanically identical to the static DiD. With 2016 Q2 normalization, every quarter is instead compared with that single quarter, so the displayed average can differ.

What the design buys—and assumes

  1. No donor-port choice: Volos or another port is not required to represent Piraeus.
  2. Port-wide shocks absorbed: quarter fixed effects remove common proportional shocks to both Piraeus segments.
  3. New identifying assumption: absent treatment, the container/non-container ratio would have remained stable apart from ordinary fluctuations.
Not a perfect placebo. Global containerization, segment-specific demand, reclassification, substitution between cargo types, or treatment spillovers into non-container activity can change the ratio even without—or because of—the concession.

Inference and pre-treatment evidence

With only two aggregate segment series, clustering by segment would mean only two clusters and would not be credible. The static regression therefore uses Newey–West HAC standard errors on the quarterly log ratio. The fully flexible quarterly event path is saturated, so its pointwise path is shown descriptively without conventional confidence intervals.

These choices make the limits visible rather than manufacturing apparent precision from two clusters.

Interpretation

This result is best treated as a mechanism/negative-control robustness test alongside the donor-port estimates. Agreement across designs would be especially persuasive; disagreement indicates that the headline result depends on the counterfactual being used.
